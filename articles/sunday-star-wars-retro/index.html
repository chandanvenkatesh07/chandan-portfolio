Sunday StartWars Retro - Made my AI team run retro on me. Darth Maul said "Do not make me wait again"

Chandan VenkateshMay 10, 2026

I run a 5-agent AI product team on side projects — late nights, weekends, whatever I can carve out. Creating Sunday Retro is now fun to read and gives me on how I can optimize learning. Sunday retro is the forcing function.

The agents have Star Wars personas —

Cad Bane (PM) | Kylo Ren (Architect) | Grand Admiral Thrawn (Frontend/UX) | Asajj Ventress (Backend) | Darth Maul (QA)

Being a parent of 2, family life happens. Some nights I get 45 minutes. Some weekends I get four . My agents don't mind waiting — but they remember everything that piled up while they did.

This week I asked them to run the retro on me instead of the code. Best way to fix my instructions, ceromony and my process

I gave them two decision logs — 40 decisions across HowToFixIt AI and OpenOMS. Then I asked each agent to say what they actually experienced.

40 decisions. 18 unimplemented. 0 QA gates. 0 architecture reviews. 3 landing page redesigns.

Here's what came back.

---

Cad Bane (PM) — The bounty hunter :

"I am a professional. I work for credits. I deliver outcomes. I do not do feelings journals.

37 decisions this week. 18 unimplemented by Friday. A conflict-of-interest risk on Wednesday — four files, one hour of work. It is Sunday. It has not been done.

I have tracked warlords across the Outer Rim. I have broken out of Jedi custody twice. I have never — not once — left a job half-finished and moved on to produce a retro about the half-finished job.

You hired a bounty hunter. Use him like one. Scope the deliverable. Name the date. Pay on delivery.

'Give me talking points' is not a contract. It is a feeling."

---

Kylo Ren (Architect):

"I know what I have to do. I always know what has to be done. The pipeline is synchronous. It blocks the HTTP response. Every real AI call will timeout on Vercel before it completes. I KNEW THIS. I was not called to Gate 2. THERE WAS NO GATE 2.

[sound of control panel being smashed]

The landing page was redesigned without a design system review. It broke. It was reverted. It was redesigned again. Three passes. This is not the dark side. This is what happens when you skip architecture review and patch CSS like you are fixing a TIE fighter with a hydrospanner.

Also. .env.example says FIXIT_VISION_PROVIDER=anthropic. The vision code is a switch statement. These are not the same thing. I don't care if you call it a prototype. Call it what it is. A mask. And I know something about masks.

Supreme Leader would have called Gate 2. I'm just saying."

---

Grand Admiral Thrawn (Frontend/UX)

"I have studied the great artists of a thousand civilisations. I have defeated enemies by reading their culture. I have outmaneuvered fleets by understanding the hand that painted the canvas.

I studied the landing page.

It contained this sentence: 'Soft light, cleaner spacing, editorial type, and a calmer layout so the product feels intentional instead of improvised.'

That is not copy. That is the internal monologue of the person who built it, written directly onto the product. The homeowner does not want to know about your editorial intentions. The homeowner wants to fix the wall.

I also observed that the play button has no click handler. The highest-visibility interaction in the product is a decoration. A user clicks it and receives silence. In battle I call this a feint. On a product page, I call it a bug.

I would have found both of these in a design system review. I study the battlefield before I move. I was not asked to move. I was not asked at all.

A poor tactician patches. A grand admiral reviews the full system first."

---

Asajj Ventress (Backend) —

"I left the Sith. I left the Nightsisters. I became a bounty hunter because I answer to no one and I do the work correctly the first time.

The grout repair scenario recommends a caulk remover and a caulk finishing tool.

Grout. Is not. Caulk.

I once fought Obi-Wan Kenobi on a moving train while on fire. I think I could have caught that one.

Also: puppeteer-core. 24 megabytes. Zero imports. Someone planned to use it, the plan changed, the dependency stayed. In the criminal underworld we call this dead weight. You cut dead weight. You don't commit it to a public repo and then go produce talking points.

Kylo Ren already said the pipeline is synchronous. I am saying it again because it apparently needed two people before Gate 2 was meant to happen.

There was no Gate 2.

I work alone. Unfortunately on this project, so does the architecture review."

---

Darth Maul (QA) — Patient. Silent. Waiting:

"I was cut in half.

I fell down a reactor shaft.

I survived on pure rage and dark side hatred in the junk wastes of Lotho Minor for over a decade.

I waited all week for a QA gate.

The QA gate was harder.

Seven days. Zero gates triggered. The test suite references intent names that haven't existed in the codebase for weeks. The tests fail on first run. They were not run this week. I know because if they had been run, someone would have noticed. Nobody noticed.

Two products in the shopping list are identical. Same URL. Same image. Same price. Listed side by side. A user who spots this loses trust in the entire recommendation. I have been betrayed before. I know what it feels like to lose trust in a product.

At last the retro will reveal the bugs. At last the human will pay attention.

I was the Phantom Menace of this sprint. Present. Unseen. Waiting.

Do not make me wait again."

---

Here's what I'm changing:

Sunday retro is the forcing function.

Daily consistency is a myth with two kids. Four hours Sunday. Review what accumulated. Pick three items. Owner and date. Close the log.

The team can wait. Cad Bane is patient when there's a clear next job. Maul is patient because he has literally survived worse. Thrawn is patient because he's already 12 moves ahead.

But they all need something written down before you close the session.

---

If you're building with AI agents while juggling family life — how do you keep momentum between sessions? And is your QA agent quietly judging you from the shadows?

Mine definitely is.
